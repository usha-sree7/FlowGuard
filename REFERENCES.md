Original columns → standard fields:

* `source_file`: dataset.zip :: bacia_rio_bengalas_2019_2023_out00h30m.csv
* `datahora`: timestamp (timezone not stated in repo - treated as naive local time)
* `nivel_conselheiro`: level [m] - water level at the hydrological station (unit inferred: the authors' code compares it with mean+2sd 'flood threshold'; values 0.50-4.44)
* `chuva_conselheiro`: rain__gauge [mm per 30 min, UNVERIFIED unit] - station rain gauge
* `precip0..precip503`: 504 weather-radar grid cells (504 records in WeatherRadarCoverage_Grid.dbf). Aggregated to rain__radar = cell mean [unit not stated in repo; bias-adjusted to the gauge on the train split]
* `out00h30m`: next-step water level (verified == nivel_conselheiro at t+30min for all 70,760 consecutive pairs). Not used as a model input.

Processed columns:

* `t_hours`: hours since the first record (derived)
* `timestamp`: record time as given in the source (empty for Malwathu: no dates in the file)
* `level`: river water level [m]
* `rain__<sensor>`: rainfall per step for each sensor (gauge / radar cell mean / station gauges); unit see notes
* `step_ok`: True when the previous record is exactly one nominal step earlier (derived)
* `rise_rate`: level change per hour over exactly one nominal step [m/h], NaN across gaps (derived)

Preprocessing steps:

1. verified SHA-256 of original download
2. read original file(s) with documented column mapping (see registry.column_mapping)
3. aggregated 504 radar cells to their arithmetic mean (rain__radar)
4. validated out00h30m == next-step level (not used as input)
5. computed t_hours, step_ok and rise_rate (only across one nominal step)
6. no rows dropped, no values imputed, no outliers removed in the processed copy

**Target variable and labels (PROXY, not recorded floods).** No observed flood/no-flood labels in the repository. The authors' uncertainty-analysis code defines a statistical 'flood threshold' = mean + 2*std of nivel_conselheiro (computed by them on the full dataset). HydroSentinel AI recomputes it on the chronological TRAIN split only and uses it as the High threshold of a 5-level PROXY label on the FUTURE water level (Normal / Low / Moderate / High / Critical). Low (mean+0.5sd), Moderate (mean+1sd) and Critical (mean+3sd) are project extensions, not from the paper. The label at horizon h is the class of the observed level exactly h steps later, computed from the original (uncorrupted) data; Alert = High or Critical.

**Splits (chronological):** fit block 40,325 rows (2019-01-04 14:30:00 to 2021-10-04 19:00:00); validation 10,082 rows (from 2021-10-04 19:30:00 to 2022-07-10 15:00:00); test 21,604 rows (2022-07-10 15:30:00 to 2023-12-31 16:00:00). Horizons: 30 min, 1 h, 3 h.

Known limitations:

* Proxy labels only: exceedance of a statistical level threshold, not observed flood impacts.
* Units of rain gauge and radar columns are not documented in the repository.
* Timezone not stated.
* 1,250 timestamp gaps longer than 30 min; rise rate is not computed across gaps.
* Single water-level sensor: level faults cannot be cross-checked against a redundant level sensor.

Not independently verified:

* Paper full text (Wiley returned HTTP 403). Authors, title, Transactions in GIS 30(2), article e70254 confirmed via the Crossref record of the DOI (2026-10-26).
* Brief states '>73,000 tuples'; the 30-min file actually has 72,011 rows (69,356 in the 6-h file).

Processed columns:

* `t_hours`: hours since the first record (derived)

Preprocessing steps:

1. verified SHA-256 of original download
2. read original file(s) with documented column mapping (see registry.column_mapping)
3. reconstructed continuous daily series from lag-structured rows (4,765 days)

5. computed t_hours, step_ok and rise_rate (only across one nominal step)

**Target variable and labels (PROXY, not recorded floods).** Paper reports no numeric flood/alert water levels. * No timestamps in the files; only years 2005-2018 are stated in the paper.
* Daily resolution: flash-flood dynamics within a day are not observable.
* Rain unit ambiguity (paper says cm; values look like mm).
* Proxy labels only; paper gives no alert levels.

Not independently verified:

* Data-provider agency not stated in the paper.
* Exact start/end dates.

## 4. Research gap (relative to the works above, not a systematic review)

The techniques are established individually. The integration studied here is per-sensor reliability assessment → interval-preserving fusion → IT2FIS, with SOM / Fuzzy ART pattern familiarity changing only the confidence status, and flood risk and confidence reported separately. The project also contributes a seeded, like-for-like comparison under 11 simulated sensor-fault scenarios and several horizons. Whether the integration helps is reported per scenario in `results/MODEL_COMPARISON_<dataset>.md`; it is not assumed.
